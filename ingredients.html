The 42 Ingredients in Mirthu Poshan Malt Flour

Explore the ingredients listed in our recipe and the nutrients or culinary qualities they contribute. Whole, split and colour variants are listed separately, as in the recipe.

Understanding these nutrition notes

These sources describe individual foods, not laboratory results for Mirthu Poshan. The amount in a serving depends on the recipe proportions, processing and preparation. This flour complements a varied diet and is not a meal replacement or a treatment for any medical condition.

Allergens: the listed recipe includes barley (gluten), peanuts, almonds, walnuts, soy and sesame. This blend is not gluten-free. Contact us about allergen handling before ordering.

Ingredient guide

Millets (8)

Kodo Millet

Carbohydrate and fibre

A cereal grain that contributes energy and dietary fibre.

Sources: ICAR–Indian Institute of Millets Research: millet names and composition

Navane (Foxtail Millet)

Carbohydrate and protein

Adds cereal carbohydrate, plant protein and fibre; preparation affects the nutritional profile.

Sources: ICAR–Indian Institute of Millets Research: millet names and composition

Baragu (Proso Millet)

Carbohydrate and protein

Contributes cereal energy and plant protein to a varied diet.

Sources: ICAR–Indian Institute of Millets Research: millet names and composition

Saame (Little Millet)

Carbohydrate and fibre

A small millet that contributes carbohydrate and dietary fibre, adding variety to cereal-based meals.

Sources: ICAR–Indian Institute of Millets Research: millet names and composition

Korale (Browntop Millet)

Carbohydrate and fibre

Adds cereal carbohydrate, plant protein and dietary fibre as one of the millets in the recipe.

Sources: ICAR–Indian Institute of Millets Research: millet names and composition

Oodhalu (Barnyard Millet)

Carbohydrate and fibre

Contributes carbohydrate, fibre and some plant protein.

Sources: ICAR–Indian Institute of Millets Research: millet names and composition

Raagi (Finger Millet)

Calcium

Contains calcium, which contributes to normal bones and teeth as part of a balanced diet.

Sources: ICAR–Indian Institute of Millets Research: millet names and composition

Jowar (Sorghum)

Carbohydrate and fibre

Contributes cereal carbohydrate, plant protein and fibre. This blend also contains barley and is not gluten-free.

Sources: ICAR–Indian Institute of Millets Research: millet names and composition

Nuts (2)

Almond

Vitamin E and unsaturated fats

Provides vitamin E and unsaturated fats; vitamin E contributes to normal antioxidant function.

Sources: NIH Office of Dietary Supplements: vitamin E food sources

Walnut

Plant omega-3 (ALA)

Contains alpha-linolenic acid (ALA), a plant omega-3 fat; ALA is different from marine EPA and DHA.

Sources: NIH Office of Dietary Supplements: omega-3 fatty acids

Seeds (8)

Pumpkin Seeds

Magnesium

Contributes magnesium, a mineral involved in normal muscle and nerve function.

Sources: NIH Office of Dietary Supplements: magnesium food sources

Cucumber Seeds

Seed oil

Cucumber seed oil contains fatty acids, including linoleic acid, that contribute to the fat content of the seeds.

Sources: Wu et al. (2012): chemical constituents of cucumber seed oil

Sunflower Seeds

Vitamin E

Provides vitamin E, which contributes to normal antioxidant function.

Sources: NIH Office of Dietary Supplements: vitamin E food sources

Watermelon Seeds

Protein and fats

Adds plant protein and fat; seed nutrients differ from those in the watery fruit pulp.

Sources: USDA FoodData Central: dried watermelon seed kernels (FDC 169407)

Poppy Seeds

Fats and minerals

Contains fat and minerals, including calcium. The amount delivered depends on the portion used.

Sources: ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017

Til (Sesame Seeds)

Unsaturated fats

Adds fat and plant protein. Its nutrient contribution to a serving depends on the amount used.

Sources: ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017

Fenugreek Seeds

Fibre and culinary flavour

Adds fibre and a characteristic flavour. Evidence is insufficient to promise digestive or blood-sugar benefits from this blend.

Sources: ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017, NIH NCCIH: fenugreek evidence and safety

Flax Seeds

Plant omega-3 (ALA)

Contributes alpha-linolenic acid, an essential plant omega-3 fatty acid.

Sources: NIH Office of Dietary Supplements: omega-3 fatty acids

Pulses and legumes (20)

Bellar (Avare Kalu)

Plant protein

Whole field beans, also called avare kalu, contribute plant protein and carbohydrate. Cook before eating.

Sources: Tamil Nadu Agricultural University: lablab / Dolichos bean identity, Tamil Nadu Agricultural University: composition of pulses

Soya Beans

Plant protein

Contributes plant protein and fat. Soy is one of the allergens in the recipe.

Sources: ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017

Kabul Gram (Kabuli Chickpeas)

Protein and fibre

Contributes plant protein and fibre. Kabuli and black chana are varieties of chickpea, not different species.

Sources: ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017

Green Gram (Mung Bean)

Protein and fibre

Adds plant protein and dietary fibre; whole and split forms have different fibre contents.

Sources: ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017

Bellar Dal (Avare Bele)

Plant protein

The split form of Bellar, called avare bele, contributes plant protein. Splitting and removal of the seed coat change its fibre content.

Sources: Tamil Nadu Agricultural University: lablab / Dolichos bean identity, Tamil Nadu Agricultural University: composition of pulses

Cow Peas White

Protein and fibre

White cowpeas contribute plant protein, carbohydrate and dietary fibre.

Sources: ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017

Cow Peas Brown

Protein and fibre

Brown cowpeas contribute plant protein, carbohydrate and dietary fibre.

Sources: ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017

Green Peas

Protein and carbohydrate

Adds plant protein and carbohydrate. Fresh and dried peas have different nutrient concentrations.

Sources: ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017

White Peas

Protein and fibre

Dried peas contribute plant protein and fibre when cooked.

Sources: Tamil Nadu Agricultural University: composition of pulses

Black Urad (Whole Black Gram)

Protein and fibre

Whole black gram contributes plant protein and fibre, including fibre from its seed coat.

Sources: ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017

Rajma White

Protein and fibre

White kidney beans contribute plant protein and fibre when properly cooked.

Sources: Tamil Nadu Agricultural University: composition of pulses

Kidney Beans Red

Protein and fibre

Red kidney beans contribute plant protein and fibre; they must be properly processed and cooked.

Sources: ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017

Kidney Beans Brown

Protein and fibre

Brown kidney beans contribute plant protein and dietary fibre when properly cooked.

Sources: ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017

Horse Gram

Protein and fibre

Adds plant protein and dietary fibre to a varied diet.

Sources: ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017

Peanuts

Protein and fats

A legume that contributes plant protein and fats. Peanut is an allergen, distinct from tree nuts.

Sources: ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017

Chick Peas (Black Chana)

Protein and fibre

Whole chickpeas contribute plant protein and fibre; black chana is a chickpea variety.

Sources: ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017

Urad Dal (Split Black Gram)

Plant protein

Split black gram contributes plant protein; removing the seed coat changes its fibre content.

Sources: ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017

Masoor Dal (Lentils)

Protein and fibre

Contributes plant protein and fibre; nutrient values depend on the whole or split form.

Sources: ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017

Moth Gram

Plant protein

Adds plant protein, carbohydrate and dietary fibre to a varied diet.

Sources: ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017

Split Bengal Gram (Chana Dal)

Plant protein

Split chickpeas contribute plant protein; their fibre content differs from whole chickpeas.

Sources: ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017

Other cereals and starches (2)

Barley (Barli)

Carbohydrate and fibre

A cereal that contributes carbohydrate and dietary fibre. Barley contains gluten and is unsuitable for a gluten-free diet.

Sources: ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition: Indian Food Composition Tables 2017

Sago (Sabudana)

Starch

Primarily contributes carbohydrate energy, with little protein or fibre. It is not a pulse or a type of rice.

Sources: USDA: Nutritive Value of Foods (Home and Garden Bulletin 72)

Spices (2)

Elachi (Cardamom)

Aromatic spice

Adds a characteristic aroma and flavour in the small quantities used as a culinary spice.

Sources: Tamil Nadu Agricultural University: culinary spices

Jeera (Cumin Seeds)

Aromatic spice

Adds a distinctive aroma and flavour in the small quantities used as a culinary spice.

Sources: Tamil Nadu Agricultural University: culinary spices
